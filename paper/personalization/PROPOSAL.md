## 1. Positioning
Paper 2 built drift machinery at the meta timescale; this paper applies it at the individual timescale: **player skill is drift, per player**. Same corpus, same hierarchical estimation habits — and, importantly, the *same drift-aware expectation model* (see §2).

**Deliverable on hotsfever:** recommendations conditioned on **full both-team identity** — "best pick for *your whole team* against *their whole team*," when all ten identities are known — via the already-spec'd leaf-adjustment (population WP at the tree leaf, adjusted by personal deltas for every identified player). Plus a degraded mode that accepts identifiers for an **arbitrary subset** of slots: your four teammates are reliably identifiable pre-game, but opponents often are not — battletags are non-unique across accounts, so opponent identity is only trustworthy where we can disambiguate via `blizz_id` (available whenever we've seen the account in refetched replay data). The product ships whatever the paper concludes, at whatever level of identity is actually available in the lobby.

**Baseline in the literature: DraftRec** (Lee et al., WWW 2022). Context for Ernest: DraftRec is personalized hero recommendation for League of Legends, trained to *imitate players' own picks* given their match histories — a user encoder feeding a draft-state transformer, optimized for pick prediction (does the model recommend what this player would have picked?), with match outcome used only as an auxiliary signal. That makes it an imitation objective, not an outcome objective — it learns what you *do*, not what *wins for you*. Our contrast: we ground personalization in **outcome-based skill residuals** and ask whether personalization improves *win probability*, not pick-prediction accuracy. Scope: **cite and conceptually contrast only — no reimplementation** unless reviewers demand it. **[Max's call, pending Ernest]**

## 2. Foundation experiment (build first)
**Draft-adjusted residuals.** Per player-game: `resid = outcome − WP_drift(draft, map, tier, t)`, where **`WP_drift` is the drift-aware, cumprev-refreshed causal WP model from paper 2 — not the frozen paper-1 model.** This matters: the expectation baseline must track the meta, or every player's "skill" estimate silently absorbs meta drift (a player who mained a hero through its nerf patch looks like they got worse). The **frozen paper-1 model is retained as an ablation arm only**, to quantify exactly how much meta drift contaminates naive residuals. Raw player-hero WR is confounded by draft quality, map, and tier — a player who only plays Cho'gall in already-losing comps looks worse than they are. Shrunken (empirical-Bayes) aggregation of residuals per player×hero replaces raw WR as the skill measure *everywhere downstream* (Max's #8). Sanity checks: residuals mean-zero by construction on the WP model's own calibration set; shrinkage weights validated by split-half reliability.

## 3. Core experiments
**(#1) Collapse test — does personalization degenerate to "everyone drafts their 3 best heroes"?** Compare the spread of per-player MAWP deltas against the spread of draft-interaction deltas (synergy/counter terms): if personal deltas dominate, the personalized policy ignores the draft. Then simulate a personalized drafter and measure per-player pick concentration (entropy / effective hero pool) vs the population policy. Direct callback to paper 1's concentration finding — this is the policy-level failure mode accuracy metrics will hide, again.

**(#2) Nested-model lift — the signal check, run first.** Population WP → + raw player-hero WR → + shrunken residual skill → (+ embeddings from #4). Accuracy and calibration on held-out games of tracked/high-density players, strictly future-split. This tells us whether personalization is worth anything predictively and how much the residual cleaning matters over naive WR. **Sequencing decision [Max's call, pending Ernest]: #2 runs first** — it's the cheap go/no-go signal for everything else — **but #1 is the featured behavioral finding** of the paper, continuing our accuracy-vs-policy thesis. If #2 shows no lift, that null plus #1's mechanism is still a paper; if it shows lift, #1 is what makes the paper ours.

**(#3) Learning curves as residual-vs-experience curves.** Residual trajectory vs games-played on a hero, hierarchical across players (paper-2 machinery at per-player timescale): how many games until a new hero reaches a player's baseline? Fully parallel to paper 2's "trust patch-local stats after N games."

**(#4) Collaborative filtering on the player×hero residual matrix.** Implicit-ALS matrix factorization as baseline; factorization machines with hero side-features (role, AA-vs-ability, range, difficulty) for interpretable structure — "good at auto-attackers, bad at tanks." **Evaluation is cold-start: "the Cassia test"** — mask a player's first N games on rarely-played heroes, predict masked residuals from the embedding + side features, score against observed. This is the experiment that makes recommendations *for heroes you haven't played* defensible. Scope decision **[Max's call, pending Ernest]:** the embedding is **one experiment among the set, not the organizing object of the paper** — the paper's spine is the residual foundation and the policy-level findings.

**(#5) Player×hero×map variance decomposition.** One mixed-effects table: variance shares of player, hero, player×hero, player×map, player×hero×map. Decides in one shot whether map-conditioning of *personal* skill matters or whether MAWP personalization can drop the map axis.

**(#6) Playstyle** — defer substance to the talent paper, but note the CF embeddings may surface playstyle axes for free; if an embedding dimension aligns with aggression/utility we flag it and move on.

**(#7) Retroactive smurf identification with a residual-purification loop.** Smurfs are not garnish; they are a *validity threat to every experiment above* — a smurf's games poison the population expectation (their opponents' residuals are unfairly negative, their own hero "learning curves" are fake). Promoted to a core experiment with an EM-style loop: (i) estimate per-player skill from residuals; (ii) flag anomalous accounts — embedding / first-N-games projection lands far above the new-account prior (e.g. first-20-game trajectory projecting to high-skill embedding regions); (iii) adjust expected WP in games containing flagged accounts; (iv) re-estimate everyone else's residuals; iterate to convergence. Deliverables: a retroactive smurf census of the ladder (interesting on its own), and **purified skill labels for #1-#5** — we report headline results on both raw and purified residuals so the purification is itself evaluated, not assumed.
