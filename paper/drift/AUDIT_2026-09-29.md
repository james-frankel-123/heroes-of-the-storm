## A. Could change a headline claim

### A1. Every "cutoff-frozen" arm trains on statistics that include the training row's own outcome

**Where:** L186-189 ("every feature is causal: no training or evaluation row ever sees statistics from ... any later build") is false for these arms. Also affected: Table I rows 1-7, L280-285, L300-335, L374-381, L561-564, L926-930, the Fig. 1 caption, and abstract L51-54.

**Mechanism:** the `cutoff` pass in `training/drift2026/build_drift_features.py` (L6-7 and L113) gives every row, training rows included, cumulative statistics through 2.55.14.95918. A December 2021 training row therefore sees hero, pair, map and composition win rates built from every game through February 2026, including itself. `MANIFEST.md` leakage rule 2 says this outright ("cutoff stats for BOTH train and test rows"). The same pattern holds for:
- all seven standard regimes (`phase_d2.py`),
- the W2c frozen-at-C0 arm (`features_cutoff_2.55.3.89754`),
- the stale-1yr and stale-2yr VFs (`w7_stale_*`),
- the `causal_*` prior (the L345-352 control).

Only `d2c_cumprev`, the decayed cells and the W2c retrain models are causal on training rows.

**Evidence (recomputed; scratch `leak_probe.py`, `reliance_probe.py`, `w2b_cross.py`):**

Swapping the feature source changes accuracy only where a row's own game can be inside its statistics:

| Model | Features | Val acc (train period) | Future acc |
|---|---|---|---|
| `d2b_allhist_s42` | its own cutoff features | 59.07 | 56.18 |
| `d2b_allhist_s42` | causal cumprev features | 55.33 | 56.28 |
| `d2c_cumprev_s42` | its own features | 57.16 | not rerun |
| `d2c_cumprev_s42` | cutoff features | 57.73 | not rerun |
| `w2c_frozenstats_cut08` (leaky C0 arm) | its own features | 61.49 | 55.13 |
| `w2c_cut08` (causal C0 arm) | its own features | 56.65 | 56.08 |

- `d2c_cumprev_s42` gains +0.57 on validation rows from label information alone.
- The per-column (cutoff minus cumprev) feature deltas correlate with the row label at |r| up to 0.059 on validation rows and at most 0.005 on future rows.

Permutation reliance, measured on future rows:
- The leak-trained VF barely uses `role_counts` (output shift 0.8-1.2 against 3.6-3.7 for cumprev).
- It leans on the leakiest columns instead: `pairwise_synergies` (5.7 vs 1.2), `counter_detail` (5.9 vs 3.0) and `map_delta` (4.7 vs 2.2). A value function that ignores role counts drafts teams with no healer or frontline.

Cross-evaluation settles where the degeneracy comes from. Using the exact W2b protocol (3 seeds × 500 drafts), the degeneracy follows the weights, not the deployment statistics:

| VF (training features) | Deployment stats | Degen % | Table I |
|---|---|---|---|
| maintained (causal) | cutoff, never refreshed | 17.2 (16.2 / 17.6 / 17.8) | 16.8 |
| all-history (leaky) | cumprev, refreshed | 44.3 (43.6 / 43.4 / 45.8) | 43.5 |

**What this changes:**
- **Section 3's thesis.** "Drift damages policies far more than accuracy reveals ... 2.5×" is, on this evidence, "training on self-inclusive aggregates damages policies". That is the paper-1 failure class, not a cost of drift. The L280-285 "ordering flips" reading (val vs future) is largely the same artifact, because the Val column is leaky for rows 1-7 and causal only for maintained.
- **The "two thirds recipe, one third refresh" split** (L562-564, L928) is mostly leaky vs causal training.
- **Table III row 1** (`unmaintained`, +0.66). This row prices "leak-trained VF + no refresh", and the stale-1yr/2yr rows use the same leaky recipe. Differences between rows still price staleness under a fixed recipe, but the recipe is leaky.
- **The mechanism sentence** "baked in at training time, by learning against statistics that tracked their own era" (L380-381) has a rival explanation it cannot exclude: learning against statistics that contain the label.

**Fix:**
- Build a leave-one-out `cutoff` pass by subtracting each row's own game from its hero, pair, map and comp counts. Alternatively, freeze all-history statistics strictly before each training row.
- Retrain the three D2 seeds (about 40 s each) and rerun W2b (3 × 500 drafts). If degeneracy stays near 43%, the drift story survives and the paper gains a strong control. If it falls toward 17%, Section 3 has to be rewritten around self-inclusion.
- Either way, correct L186-189 so that it says exactly what is causal. Flag the Val column. Add a Limitations sentence.
- Ideally, rerun the Table III unmaintained agent from a leave-one-out VF. That needs 5 MCTS seeds (about 5 h on 4 GPUs) plus the h2h.

### A2. The head-to-head "maintained" agent is a policy retrained with fresh statistics, so it does not show "refresh with weights fixed"

**Where:** L106-108, L209-211, L239 (names table), L552-557, L607-616, abstract L54-60, conclusion L944-946.

**Mechanism:** in `w6_head2head.py` (`make_actor`) the agents draft greedily from their policy heads. The input is `DraftState.to_numpy()` (`train_draft_policy.py` L87-96): picks, bans, map, tier and step. It contains no aggregate statistics.

Statistics reach an agent only during MCTS training, through the kernel LUT (`MCTS_STATS_BUILD`, `train_mcts_worker.py` L163-172):
- The maintained agents were trained from scratch (300K episodes) with statistics through 2.55.16.96881 (`phase_w4_mcts.cell_stats_build` returns `builds[-2]`).
- The unmaintained agents were trained with statistics at the cutoff.

So Table III compares "policy re-trained with May-2026 statistics" against "policy trained with February-2026 statistics". At the agent level, maintenance meant a full policy retrain.

**What this changes:**
- The abstract ties the +0.66 result to the "cheapest repair is data-side" story. Nothing in the head-to-head shows a fixed-weight refresh working for an agent.
- L239 ("its drafting statistics are three months older") is false. Drafting uses no statistics; the difference is in the statistics used to *train* the policy.

**Fix:**
- State the protocol plainly: "the maintained agent's policy network is trained by MCTS against refreshed statistics; at play time both agents act greedily from their policy heads without statistics."
- Rescope the data-side claims to the value-function and accuracy evidence (Tables I and IV).
- Optional experiment: run both agents *with search* at draft time (MCTS with the kernel LUT), swapping only the LUT statistics. That would be the agent-level version of "refresh with weights fixed".

### A3. "Refresh beats retraining" (title, abstract L60-62, L106-108, L357-358, conclusion L949-951) is contradicted by Table IV

In the replay (`W2C_SUMMARY.md`):
- Refresh alone reaches +0.730 regret.
- Every refresh+retrain cadence reaches +0.029 to +0.308.
- Retraining on top of refresh is therefore worth 0.73pp.

Same-model refresh alone is worth only 1.063 → 0.730 = **0.33pp** (`DETECTOR_CURVES.md`). The +1.682 row is a different model: the leaky frozen-feature recipe of A1.

Q5 (`PARTIAL_REFRESH.md`) points the same way. Test-time refresh on fixed `d2c_cumprev` checkpoints is worth +0.030pp (game-clustered SE 0.015). The paper itself says most of the gain is "baked in at training time" (L379-381).

No replay arm retrains *without* refreshing, and none of the Table I regimes is a retraining *schedule*; they are one-cutoff recipes.

**False sentences that follow from this:**
- **L106-108:** "Keeping the model's weights fixed and recomputing ... beats every retraining schedule we tested."
- **L340-341:** "5.13 to 6.08 with refresh alone, a 19% larger edge." Same-model refresh takes the edge from 5.75 to 6.08, about 6%. The 19% is recipe plus refresh.
- **L795-796:** "Refresh alone recovers more than half of that without touching the weights." Same-model refresh recovers 0.333 of 1.063, which is 31%.
- **L949-951:** "retraining adds little once the statistics are fresh." Retraining adds 0.73pp, more than twice what refresh adds on the same model.
- **L157-158 and L797:** "every reasonable retraining cadence lands within 0.1pp." This holds for K ≤ 6 only. K=9 gives 0.199, K=12 gives 0.222, and the 1.0pp trigger gives 0.308.

**Fix:**
- Retitle, for example "Maintaining Draft Policies Under Meta Drift: Causal Aggregates, Refresh, and When to Retrain", or "Refresh the Aggregates, Then Retrain on a Schedule".
- Restate the finding in one line: "training on era-aligned causal aggregates carries most of the gain; refreshing them adds about 0.3pp over 2.8 years at no training cost; retraining every 3-6 builds closes the remaining 0.7pp, and within that range the cadence barely matters."
- Add a model column to Table IV, or footnote that rows 2 and 3 are different models.

### A4. The detector-triggered refresh matches a blind calendar schedule as well as it matches every-build refresh

**Where:** abstract L63-65, L124-126, L515-521, L798-800.

The paper compares 14 detector refreshes only against 35 blind refreshes. The replay audit added blind baselines on the same C0 model (scratch `blind_k.py`, `blind_sched.py`, `sched_eval.py`; the pipeline reproduces 56.080 and 55.748 exactly):

| Schedule | Refreshes | Regret |
|---|---|---|
| Blind, every 3rd build | 11 | +0.738 |
| Blind, 14 evenly spaced | 14 | +0.748 |
| Detector | 14 | +0.743 |
| Every build | 35 | +0.730 |
| 1 to 3 blind refreshes, year-scale spacing | 1-3 | +0.843 to +0.917 |
| Never | 0 | +1.063 |

**The "40% of the refreshes" figure** divides by 35 builds. 16 of those builds have fewer than 20K games and 8 have fewer than 5K. Over the 19 sizable, non-no-op builds, the detector fires 14 times (74%).

**Refreshes cost almost nothing anyway.** Recomputing aggregates is cheap, so saving 60% of them is not a practical selling point.

**Fix:**
- Add the blind rows to Table IV.
- Pitch the detector on its own merits: 16 of 21 changed boundaries fire against 1 of 6 no-change boundaries, it works as an alarm, and it works in domains without a release cadence. Do not pitch it as a cost saving.
- Drop "the loop closes" and "the trigger works outside the lab framing". Drop "40%", or restate it over sizable builds.

### A5. The head-to-head inference replicates MCTS seeds only; the value function is a single seed per arm, picked on the test window

`phase_w4_mcts.export_vf` (L102), `w7_staleness.export_stale_vf` (L53) and the volmatch/champion exports each select **one** VF seed as the best of 3 by `test_acc_future`. That is accuracy on the 7 post-cutoff builds, which include the first week of 2.55.16.97039, now part of the clean scoring set.

| Arm | Chosen seed | Its future acc | Other seeds |
|---|---|---|---|
| champion | `q7_decayed90k100_s777` | 57.303 | 57.16, 57.20 |
| cumprev | s123 | 57.114 | 57.09, 57.04 |
| allhist | s777 | 56.274 | 56.17, 56.18 |

All 5 or 15 "seeds per side" share one VF, so the crossed-RE SE (L580-581, "charges seed variance properly") conditions on a single draw of the unit that carries the treatment. That is pseudo-replication at the level the paper itself says matters: "claims about agents need agent-level seed budgets" (L408).

It also inflates W11 slightly. The champion VF sits +0.19 above the chosen cumprev VF on the selection metric.

**Fix:**
- Disclose the selection rule and that inference is conditional on the VF.
- For the headline (Table III row 1) and W11, train MCTS agents on a second VF seed per arm (2 VF × 5 MCTS seeds) and report the VF-seed component.
- Do not select VF seeds on test-window accuracy.

### A6. Every MCTS agent, including the "two-year-stale" one, is bootstrapped from and trained against a 2026 opponent model that saw the test period

The W4/W7/W8/W10 recipes set `MCTS_GD_PATH = rerun2026/models/generic_draft_0.pt`, the paper-1 full-snapshot GD trained through May 2026 (`w7_staleness.py` L85, `w8_hardening.py` L52). This model is used for two things:
- It initializes the policy head. `bootstrap_from_generic_draft` copies the GD's final layer (`train_draft_policy.py` L535-565).
- It is the in-kernel opponent throughout self-play.

On top of that, the value-head pretraining exclusion (`build_exclude_ids`) removes only post-February-2026 replays. The stale-2yr agent's value head was therefore pretrained on games from 2024 through 2026. This part matters less, because only the policy head acts in the head-to-head.

So "stale-2yr" is stale in its VF and statistics only. Its behavioral prior and its sparring partner are 2026-era and future-inclusive. This probably biases the hero-timing gap *down*, which is conservative. It directly undermines the two claims that rest on the stale agent's *interaction* play:
- "synergy knowledge does not rot" (abstract L59-60, L138-141, L627-630),
- "the stale agent counters slightly better" (L139-140, L630-637).

Counter-play is exactly what an agent learns from its opponent model.

**Fix:**
- Disclose the recipe in Section 7.
- For the two-year claim, retrain the stale-2yr MCTS arm with a GD trained through its own cutoff (5 seeds, about 5 h) and rerun the h2h.
- Without that, soften to "we detect no synergy gap at two years".

### A7. "Synergy knowledge does not rot" is an absence of evidence with a wide interval

Two-year synergy is +0.071 ± 0.159 (`W12_CLEAN_TRUTH.md`; Satterthwaite df ≈ 8.9, scratch `satt.json`). The 95% interval is roughly −0.29 to +0.43, which includes the four-month gap (+0.23) and the one-year gap (+0.25). The data cannot tell "no rot" apart from "the same gap as at four months".

Two other qualifiers:
- The W2b greedy protocol shows maintained *worse* on synergy than every standard regime (0.23 vs 0.47-0.56; `W2B_FILL.md`).
- A6 applies here too.

**Fix:** "at two years we detect no synergy gap (+0.07 ± 0.16); the interval does not exclude a gap as large as the four-month one."

### A8. The "thirty-day rolling window" opponent model was never rolled, and it is not what production runs

**Where:** L155, Table tab:gdpools, L744-750, L818-821.

`w5_gd_drift.py` L131-132 trains `win30d` **once**, on the 30 days before the cutoff (about Jan 11 to Feb 10, 2026). It is never re-rolled during the test builds. Its lead is a short-horizon effect (`W3_GD_DRIFT.md`):

| Test builds | win30d top-1 | Comparison |
|---|---|---|
| 2.55.15 builds | 13.12, 12.55, 12.18 | best pool |
| 2.55.16 builds | 11.05, 11.06, 10.80, 10.61 | worst pool; causal cutoff pool scores 10.97 on 96881 and 11.16 on 97039 |

win30d wins only on bans (17.13 vs 16.18). On picks it loses (8.65 vs 9.02 leaky, 9.04 win6).

Production does not match the text either. `training/production_refresh/refresh.py` `phase_gd` (L382-395) retrains GD on the **full** corpus every month. The value side is also a monthly cron (L410-412). That contradicts L153-161 and L818-821 ("refresh every build or on detector fire ... opponent model on a rolling month ... This is what our production deployment now runs").

**Fix:**
- Describe it as "a model trained on the 30 days before the cutoff: best for about two months, then below the cutoff pool."
- Present continuous refresh as an inference. The alternative is to actually roll the window: retrain on the last 30 days before each test build and score that build. This is cheap.
- Make the production sentences match `refresh.py`, or change production.

### A9. Abstract detector claim: 0.94 boundary precision is close to the base rate

21 of 27 boundaries carry changes, so random firing already scores 0.78. A binomial test of 16/17 against 0.78 gives p = 0.08 (one-sided).

The real evidence is the 2×2 table: 16/21 changed boundaries fire against 1/6 no-change boundaries (Fisher one-sided p = 0.015). That rests on 6 controls, and 5 of them are consecutive 2.55.3 builds from one maintenance era.

With the stricter rule that at least one flagged hero must be in the notes, boundary precision is 14/17 = 0.82: at 2.55.2.88122 all 3 flags are outside truth, and at 2.55.10.94387 the single flag is.

The hero-level lift is stronger: 0.79 (or 0.67; see B1) against a chance rate of about 0.30.

**Fix (abstract L63-64, L120-121, L494-495):** lead with the hero-level lift and the fire-rate contrast (16/21 vs 1/6, Fisher p). Say that the controls are few and clustered in one era.

### A10. "Refresh value tracks what the model leans on, not what drifts fastest" is not identified

**Where:** L113-116, L427-440, L448-451.

Raw pair win rates drift as fast as hero win rate (r_adj at lag 16: pair_with 0.617, pair_against 0.611, hero 0.622; `SIGNAL_DECAY.md`). Counter and synergy features are normalized by per-hero WR (`phase_q5_partial_refresh.py` header). That creates two problems:
- In the `pair_only` refresh, refreshed raw pair WRs over frozen hero WRs carry *current hero strength*.
- In `hero_only`, a mismatched numerator and denominator push inputs off the training manifold, which alone could explain the −0.07pp.

All effects are within ±0.07pp (the pair-only gain is +0.030, z ≈ 2.0). The matched-clock "follows its pair clock" result has the same confound.

**Fix:** restate as "refreshing the pair/composition family, which also carries hero-strength information through raw pair win rates, captures the (small) gain". Drop "the fastest-drifting signal in the game is also its least load-bearing" (L439-440), or add a cell that refreshes pair WRs together with their normalizing hero WRs.

### A11. The "detection and repair arrive on the same timescale" argument rests on a circular settling metric

**Where:** L126-128, L457-459, L508-513.

The 10,700 median is a "within 1pp of the build's *final* value" crossing. The early games are part of the final value, so it converges by construction (`W3_RECOVERY.md` says so). The observed vs-final MAE equals the binomial expectation at every checkpoint (1.20/1.20 at 8K, 0.91/0.95 at 12K). That is sample accumulation, not settling.

The deployment-honest disjoint-remainder median is **17,241** games.

The latency side is also optimistic:
- The 7,000 comes from an uncorrected per-hero p < 0.05 re-test at each day boundary, computed only over the 46 eventual true positives.
- With Bonferroni correction the median is 18,873, and 7 of the 46 never cross.
- "Heroes it confirms first" (L122) is a median over all true-positive heroes. The per-boundary first-detection median is 2,529.

**Fix:** use 17,200 for settling and a corrected latency. Then soften the matching-timescale claim or drop it, along with "By the time you can prove something changed, the data to respond with already exists."
